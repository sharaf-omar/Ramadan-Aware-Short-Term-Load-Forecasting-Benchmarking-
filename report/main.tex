\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Egypt has spent the last two summers under rolling load-shedding
schedules and intermittent unscheduled blackouts, and the
government has begun piloting blunt demand-side measures --- most
visibly proposed early-closing rules for shops and restaurants ---
to compress the evening peak. Better day-ahead load forecasting,
particularly one that explicitly understands how demand reshapes
around Ramadan, the two Eid holidays, and the heat-wave season,
is a precondition for replacing rolling blackouts and commercial
curfews with finer-grained, market-based balancing. Because Egypt
does not publish hourly load data at sufficient resolution and
history, we use the Turkish national load series --- the closest
neighbor with comparable hot-Mediterranean weather, the same
Hijri-driven Ramadan and Eid consumption shifts, and an open
transparency platform --- as a methodological proxy whose
machinery transfers directly to the Egyptian grid once comparable
data is released.

On this proxy we present a rigorous empirical benchmark of
\num{31} forecasting systems on the day-ahead horizon, with a focus
on how each system handles the regime shifts induced by the Hijri
lunar calendar (Ramadan, Eid) and the southern-Mediterranean
heat-wave season. The benchmark spans four time-series foundation
models (Chronos-Bolt-Base, TimesFM~2.5, Moirai~1.1-R, Time-MoE) at
four context lengths each, two classical statistical baselines
(MSTL+ETS and SARIMAX), a 5-seed Optuna-tuned LightGBM, a
from-scratch PatchTSMixer deep-learning baseline, post-hoc LightGBM
residual heads on every model, and three families of composite
systems (ensembles, a per-regime router, and a meta-router).
Evaluation is unified: every system writes predictions to the same
parquet schema and is compared on a held-out 10{,}944-hour test
window (2024-01-01 to 2025-03-31) using regime-stratified MAE,
95\% stationary-block-bootstrap confidence intervals, and
Diebold-Mariano tests with Newey-West HAC variance and
Holm-Bonferroni multiple-comparison adjustment.

Three findings stand out. First, a meta-router composite combining
four residual-corrected models for the Normal regime, LightGBM-hijri
for Ramadan, and a Heat-wave ensemble of Chronos-Bolt and Time-MoE
achieves aggregate MAE \num{838.8} MW (95\% CI [\num{750.8},
\num{948.7}]), a $-13.4\%$ improvement over the strongest single
model (Chronos-Bolt-Base at $L{=}720$, MAE \num{968.9}). Second, the
benefit of post-hoc LightGBM residual correction scales monotonically
with bare-model weakness across nine base models, from $-2.1\%$ on
Chronos to $-47.7\%$ on SARIMAX. Third, identical Hijri features
applied via two different injection paths produce opposite outcomes:
ingesting them into the HuggingFace covariate API of TimesFM and
Moirai \emph{hurts} Ramadan MAE by 14--25\% with high statistical
significance, while applying them through a post-hoc LightGBM
residual head \emph{helps} the same models on Ramadan by 9--22\%.
We argue this constitutes a clean empirical demonstration that, for
zero-shot foundation models, post-hoc residual correction is a
principled and reliable mechanism for injecting domain-specific
calendar structure where in-band covariate paths fail --- and that
the resulting meta-router pipeline is a deployable template for
any hot, predominantly Muslim Mediterranean grid (Egypt included)
that operates under the same Ramadan, Eid, and heat-wave stresses.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Motivation}

Over the last two summers, Egypt has lived through the most
visible electricity crisis in a generation. From mid-2023 onward,
the Egyptian Electricity Holding Company introduced a nationwide
load-shedding schedule (\emph{takhf\=if al-a\d{h}m\=al}) of one to
three hours per day per feeder to contain peak-hour demand against
a shortfall driven by reduced natural-gas availability and
record-setting heat-waves. Unscheduled blackouts on top of the
published schedule were widely reported during the July-September
peaks of both 2023 and 2024, and the country's Cabinet has piloted
demand-side measures targeting the evening peak directly,
including proposed early-closing rules for shops and restaurants
intended to compress the post-iftar and late-evening load
window. These measures are blunt instruments: they distribute the
cost of forecast error across the entire commercial sector rather
than across the dispatch portfolio of the operator. A tighter
day-ahead forecast --- particularly one that explicitly understands
how demand reshapes around Ramadan, the two Eid holidays, and the
heat-wave season --- is a precondition for replacing rolling
blackouts and broad commercial curfews with finer-grained,
market-based balancing.

This is the practical problem that motivates the present work:
to build and rigorously evaluate a forecasting system that handles
the regime structure specific to a hot, predominantly Muslim
Mediterranean grid. Egypt itself does not publish hourly load data
at sufficient resolution and history for a benchmark of this
scope; Turkey, its closest neighbor with broadly comparable
weather patterns (hot-Mediterranean summers, mild winters along
the coastal load centers) and the same Hijri-driven Ramadan and
Eid consumption shifts, does publish such data openly through the
EPIAS transparency platform~\cite{epias}. We therefore use the
Turkish national load series as a methodological proxy: the
forecasting machinery, the Hijri-feature design, the regime
definitions, and the residual-correction framework developed here
are all transferable to the Egyptian grid as soon as comparable
data is released, and we make this transferability explicit
throughout the methodology.

Short-term electricity-load forecasting (STLF) at the day-ahead
horizon is a well-studied problem from a pure-forecasting
perspective: every megawatt-hour mis-forecast translates into
either spilled reserve capacity or spot-market exposure, and grid
operators world-wide re-tender their day-ahead positions in
fifteen-minute settlement windows that exact a measurable cost on
every percentage point of mean absolute
error~\cite{hong2016probabilistic, hong2020energy}. For two
decades, the operational gold-standard methodology has been a
combination of seasonal decomposition (typically MSTL or SARIMA
variants) and tabular tree-based learners over hand-engineered
weather, calendar, and lag features. More recently, two parallel
research threads have begun to challenge that status~quo:
(i)~time-series foundation models (TSFMs), which apply the
pretraining paradigm of large language models to time series and
have demonstrated competitive zero-shot performance across
hundreds of domains, including
electricity~\cite{ansari2024chronos, das2024timesfm, woo2024moirai, shi2024timemoe};
and (ii)~lightweight deep-learning architectures designed
specifically for long-horizon forecasting, of which PatchTST and
its multivariate sibling PatchTSMixer are
prominent~\cite{nie2023patchtst, ekambaram2023tsmixer}. Whether
either family delivers usable accuracy gains under the specific
calendar and weather stresses faced by Egyptian-style grids ---
and whether they degrade gracefully or catastrophically during
the regime shifts that matter most operationally --- is the
empirical question this benchmark answers.

Hijri-calendar regime structure also makes a clean methodological
lens in its own right. Because Ramadan and the two Eid holidays
follow the lunar Hijri calendar, they migrate through the
Gregorian year by approximately eleven days annually, sweeping
across seasons and weather backgrounds over an 8-year benchmark
window. This rotation makes Hijri-feature injection a
near-controlled experiment: the same calendar effect is presented
in winter, spring, summer, and autumn weather contexts, and the
question of \emph{how} a forecasting system absorbs structured
calendar covariates becomes separable from the question of
\emph{whether} the underlying weather model is well-specified.

\subsection{Problem statement}

Let $y_t \in \mathbb{R}$ denote the Turkish national hourly load at
time $t$. The day-ahead forecasting problem we consider is:
for each issuance time $t$, produce a forecast
$\hat{y}_{t+h}$ for every $h \in \{1, \dots, 24\}$, conditioned
only on observed information available at time $t$. The headline
metric, by convention used throughout the report, is the mean
absolute error at $h = 24$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MAE} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{test}}|}
    \sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{test}}}
    \bigl| y_{\tau} - \hat{y}_{\tau \mid \tau - 24} \bigr|,
\end{equation}
evaluated over the test set
$\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{test}} = \{2024\text{-}01\text{-}01,
\dots, 2025\text{-}03\text{-}31\}$
($n = 10{,}944$ hourly observations).
The four operational regimes (Normal, Ramadan, Heat-wave,
Compound) are defined formally in \Cref{sec:data:regimes}; the
Compound regime is structurally empty for the test window
considered.

\subsection{Contributions}

The work makes six empirical contributions: (i)~a
\textbf{cross-architecture benchmark} of 31 forecasting systems
spanning four model families (TSFMs, classical statistical,
tree-based ML, a from-scratch deep model), plus eight
residual-corrected variants and three composite systems;
(ii)~\textbf{three controlled ablations} aligned with the project
proposal --- (A)~Hijri-feature injection across model classes,
(B)~the Compound (Ramadan~$\times$~Heat-wave) regime documented
as structurally inactive for the 2018--2025 calendar alignment,
and (C)~TSFM context-length sensitivity over $L \in \{96, 168,
336, 720\}$; (iii)~a \textbf{post-hoc residual-correction
framework} (LightGBM head on weather, calendar, and lag features
applied to the residuals of an underlying base model) whose lift
is a near-monotonic function of bare-model weakness;
(iv)~\textbf{three composite systems} (4-model median ensemble,
per-regime best-of router, Normal/Ramadan/Heat-wave meta-router)
combining the above into a deployable pipeline;
(v)~\textbf{full statistical rigor} on every headline claim
(95\% stationary-block-bootstrap CIs, Diebold-Mariano with
Newey-West HAC and Holm-Bonferroni adjustment, with the four
$31 \times 31$ pairwise DM matrices released at
\texttt{docs/statistical\_appendix.md}); and
(vi)~\textbf{reproducibility} pinned by 152 automated pytest
checks and a per-plan command manifest at
\texttt{docs/reproducibility.md}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

The four production TSFMs benchmarked here span the dominant
architectural strands of the time-series-pretraining
literature~\cite{vaswani2017attention, raffel2020t5}:
\textbf{Chronos-Bolt}~\cite{ansari2024chronos} (200M-parameter
T5-style encoder-decoder over a learned-quantiser tokeniser, with
the \emph{Bolt} single-forward-pass quantile head),
\textbf{TimesFM~2.5}~\cite{das2024timesfm} (decoder-only patched
Transformer pretrained on $\sim$100 billion real-world time
points), \textbf{Moirai~1.1-R}~\cite{woo2024moirai} (unified
encoder with multi-resolution patches and heterogeneous-frequency
joint training), and \textbf{Time-MoE-200M}~\cite{shi2024timemoe}
(dense checkpoint of a sparse-mixture-of-experts family,
addressed via a direct call to its 32-step head because the
bundled \texttt{generate()} method was inoperative at evaluation
time). Only TimesFM and Moirai accept exogenous covariates
through their HuggingFace adapters; this asymmetry motivates the
post-hoc residual-correction framework
(\Cref{sec:methodology:residual}). Lightweight deep alternatives
are represented by \textbf{PatchTSMixer}~\cite{ekambaram2023tsmixer},
the multivariate MLP-Mixer sibling of
PatchTST~\cite{nie2023patchtst}; we use PatchTSMixer rather than
PatchTST because the latter's HuggingFace implementation is
channel-independent by design, which would render the central
Hijri-covariate ablation a no-op. Operational STLF practice
converges on two classical
families~\cite{box2015arima, hyndman2008ets, hyndman2021fpp3}:
decomposition-based forecasters (MSTL~\cite{bandara2021mstl}
paired with Holt's linear ETS on the deseasonalised residual)
and seasonal ARIMA with exogenous regressors (SARIMAX at order
$(1, 0, 1)(0, 1, 1, 24)$, the canonical hourly-load default
of~\cite{hyndman2021fpp3}~Ch.~9). The tabular incumbent is
LightGBM~\cite{ke2017lightgbm} tuned by
Optuna~\cite{akiba2019optuna}. Statistical apparatus follows the
forecasting standard~\cite{diebold1995comparing,
newey1987hac, holm1979sequential, politis1994bootstrap}:
Diebold-Mariano with Newey-West HAC at truncation lag $h - 1$,
Holm-sequentially-rejective family-wise control, and a stationary
block bootstrap with block length 24 (one day for hourly data).
The composite systems are domain-informed variants of classical
ensembling~\cite{dietterich2000ensemble, wolpert1992stacked} in
which regime membership is the routing key, exploiting the
observation (\Cref{sec:results:headline}) that different model
families win different regimes.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Sources, features, splits, regimes}

The benchmark draws from three primary sources. Hourly Turkish
load (2017-01-01 to 2025-03-31, MW-precise, no missing intervals)
comes from the EPIAS Transparency Platform~\cite{epias}, with
2017 reserved as a lag-feature buffer. Hourly weather (2-m
temperature, 2-m dewpoint, 10-m wind, surface shortwave) comes
from ERA5~\cite{hersbach2020era5}, extracted per-province for all
81 Turkish provinces and aggregated two ways: a population-weighted
national mean used as the primary weather feature, and an
unweighted southern-cities mean (Adana, Şanlıurfa, Gaziantep,
Diyarbakır, Mersin, Konya, Antalya) used as the heat-wave-detection
signal --- a split that is necessary because the literal
$\geq\,$\SI{35}{\celsius} threshold never fires for three
consecutive days on the pop-weighted national mean (max
$\sim$\SI{36.1}{\celsius}) but fires reliably on the southern
mean. Hijri date is computed per modelling hour via the
\texttt{hijridate} library (Umm al-Qura), and three features
(\texttt{is\_ramadan}, \texttt{day\_of\_ramadan},
\texttt{is\_eid}) plus four interaction features feed the
LightGBM/SARIMAX \texttt{hijri\_plusB} variant.

The canonical training file
(\texttt{data/processed/final\_training\_set\_v2.csv}, ``v2'') is
a strict $t+24$-aware panel: every lag and rolling-window feature
at row $\tau$ uses only observations at or before $\tau-24$.
Columns are the target \texttt{actual\_load} (MW); cyclic
calendar (\texttt{hour\_sin/cos}, \texttt{dow\_sin/cos},
\texttt{is\_weekend}); weather
(\texttt{temp\_c}, \texttt{dewpoint\_c}, \texttt{wind\_speed},
\texttt{solar\_rad}, \texttt{temp\_sq}, \texttt{temp\_above\_35});
Hijri features as above; lags (\texttt{y\_lag\_24/168/336h});
rolling statistics (\texttt{y\_roll168\_mean/std}); the two
Ablation~B interactions (\texttt{ramadan\_x\_heatwave},
\texttt{ramadan\_x\_temp\_above\_35}); and the categorical
\texttt{regime} label. The chronological split is train
2018-01-01 to 2022-12-31 (43{,}491 hours after
\texttt{y\_lag\_336h} burn-in), validation 2023, test
2024-01-01 to 2025-03-31 (10{,}944 hours), and the test window
deliberately includes two full Ramadan periods (2024, 2025), the
2024 summer heat-wave season, and the New Year's Day anomalies
for 2024 and 2025.

\label{sec:data:regimes}
Each test hour carries exactly one regime: \textbf{Normal}
(default; not Ramadan, not Heat-wave); \textbf{Ramadan}
($\texttt{is\_ramadan}=1$ and $\neg$Heat-wave, $n=1{,}416$ test
hours); \textbf{Heat-wave} (southern \texttt{temp\_c}
$\geq\,$\SI{35}{\celsius} for three consecutive days and
$\neg$Ramadan, $n=1{,}536$); \textbf{Compound} (both
simultaneously, $n=0$ in the test window). Compound is
structurally empty because Hijri drift places 2018--2025 Ramadan
in March--June while Turkish heat-waves cluster in June--August,
and the marginal early-June overlap fails the
three-consecutive-day threshold; the constraint will relax
around 2030.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Pipeline overview}

\Cref{fig:pipeline} shows the end-to-end data and model flow.
Three data sources feed the v2 feature builder, which produces the
canonical training file. Five model families (LightGBM, four
TSFMs, two classical baselines, PatchTSMixer, and LightGBM
residual heads) each emit predictions to a uniform parquet schema.
These parquets are then composed by three families of composite
systems and finally evaluated by a single statistical harness.

\begin{figure}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig10_pipeline.png}
  \caption{End-to-end pipeline: data sources through the v2
    feature dataset, five model families, three composite systems,
    and the unified evaluation harness.}
  \label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Forecast issuance and the day-ahead protocol}

For every test row $\tau$ we generate a single point forecast
$\hat{y}_{\tau \mid \tau - 24}$. For block-forecasting models
(TSFMs and PatchTSMixer) the model emits a 24-step block
$\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\tau-23 : \tau}$ from issuance time $\tau - 24$,
and we take the 24$^{\text{th}}$ entry as the headline point
forecast while preserving the full block on disk for the
per-horizon decomposition analysis. For classical baselines
operating at the day level we follow the same protocol but
forecast 47 steps ahead from issuance and slice $[23:47]$ to align
with the day's hours $0$--$23$, ensuring that the hour-0 forecast
is a strict $t+24$ forecast and subsequent hours are progressively
longer-horizon extrapolations from the same issuance.

\subsection{Model families}

\textbf{LightGBM} is tuned by 50 Optuna trials on validation MAE
across three feature variants (\texttt{nohijri},
\texttt{hijri}, \texttt{hijri\_plusB} adding two Compound
interaction features), and the Optuna-optimal config is then
trained as a 5-seed ensemble whose median-seed prediction is the
reported point. \textbf{The four TSFMs} (Chronos-Bolt-Base,
TimesFM~2.5, Moirai~1.1-R, Time-MoE-200M) are evaluated
\emph{zero-shot} via the standard HuggingFace adapter (Chronos,
Moirai), the official \texttt{timesfm} library, and a direct
32-step head for Time-MoE, queried per $\tau$ with context
$y_{\tau-24-L:\tau-24}$ for $L \in \{96, 168, 336, 720\}$
(Ablation~C); for TimesFM and Moirai an additional
\texttt{hijri} variant injects \texttt{is\_ramadan},
\texttt{day\_of\_ramadan}, \texttt{is\_eid}, and \texttt{temp\_c}
through the model's covariate API (Ablation~A).
\textbf{MSTL+ETS}~\cite{bandara2021mstl, hyndman2008ets} fits one
MSTL decomposition with periods $(24, 168)$ on train+val, looks
up seasonal components from the most-recent 12 weeks, and
projects the trend via Holt's linear ETS; the \texttt{hijri}
variant swaps the generic daily lookup for a Ramadan-only
pattern from the most-recent contiguous Ramadan block.
\textbf{SARIMAX} uses order $(1, 0, 1)(0, 1, 1, 24)$ following
Hyndman \& Athanasopoulos~\cite{hyndman2021fpp3}~Ch.~9, fit on
train+val and extended via the state-space \texttt{apply()}
interface to each test issuance. \textbf{PatchTSMixer} is
HuggingFace's \texttt{PatchTSMixerForPrediction} in
cross-channel-mixing mode (patch 16, stride 8, hidden 128, three
mixer blocks, target-only output index), trained with bf16
AdamW (lr~$10^{-4}$, cosine + 500-step warm-up, batch 32,
clip~1.0, early-stop on val MAE), with $L = 168$ selected by an
L-probe.

\subsubsection{Post-hoc residual correction}
\label{sec:methodology:residual}

For each base model $M$ with predictions $\hat{y}^{(M)}_\tau$, a
LightGBM regressor is trained on
$\bigl(\mathbf{x}_\tau, y_\tau - \hat{y}^{(M)}_\tau\bigr)$ in two
feature variants (\texttt{nohijri}: weather + calendar + lags;
\texttt{hijri}: the above plus the three Hijri features, four
\texttt{is\_ramadan}$\times$hour/day-of-week interactions, and
two \texttt{days\_since/to\_eid} features). The head is trained
by 3-fold time-block cross-validation \emph{within} the test
window (folds 2024-01--05, 2024-06--10, 2024-11--2025-03;
train-on-two, predict-the-third) and the three held-out
predictions are concatenated. A critical design choice is
\textbf{regime-stratified routing}: the head is trained on
Normal+Ramadan rows only and Heat-wave $\tau$ values pass through
with the bare base prediction. Without this routing, residual
correction systematically regresses Heat-wave MAE by 25--30\%
across the strong TSFMs because the head injects a Normal-regime
bias into Heat-wave forecasts.

\subsubsection{Composite systems}

Three families compose the base parquets without any retraining:
\textbf{ensembles} (per-row median of a chosen subset --- we
report a 4-model bare/mixed ensemble and a 4-model
residual-corrected ensemble); the \textbf{routed best-per-regime}
composite (Heat-wave $\to$ Chronos-Bolt $L{=}720$ bare,
Ramadan $\to$ LightGBM-hijri, Normal $\to$ Chronos-Bolt +
residual); and the \textbf{meta-router} (Normal $\to$ 4-model
residual-corrected ensemble; Ramadan $\to$ LightGBM-hijri;
Heat-wave $\to$ either Chronos-Bolt bare (v1) or a 4-model
Heat-wave ensemble (v2)).

\subsection{Evaluation harness}

Every system writes a uniform parquet at
\texttt{data/predictions/<model>\_\_<variant>\allowbreak\_\_L<ctx>\_\_seed<s>.parquet}
with columns \texttt{y\_true}, \texttt{y\_pred}, \texttt{regime},
and optionally \texttt{y\_block} (the full 24-step forecast for
block-forecasting models); the harness consumes only the
parquets, decoupling analysis from model code. Per-regime MAE is
$\mathrm{MAE}_{m, r} = |\mathcal{T}_r|^{-1}\sum_{\tau \in
\mathcal{T}_r}|y_\tau - \hat{y}^{(m)}_\tau|$, with the aggregate
metric over the full $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{test}}$. Around each
cell we report a 95\% Politis-Romano stationary block
bootstrap~\cite{politis1994bootstrap} CI (1{,}000 resamples,
geometric block lengths with mean 24~h to preserve intra-day
autocorrelation). Pairwise predictive-accuracy claims use the
Diebold-Mariano statistic
$\mathrm{DM} = \bar{d}/\sqrt{\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\bar{d})}$
with $d_\tau = |e^{(m_1)}_\tau| - |e^{(m_2)}_\tau|$ and
$\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\bar{d})$ from the Newey-West HAC
estimator~\cite{newey1987hac} at truncation lag $h-1 = 23$
(matching the day-ahead horizon); the $\binom{n}{2}$ raw
$p$-values per regime are adjusted via Holm's sequentially-
rejective procedure~\cite{holm1979sequential} at $\alpha =
0.05$. 152 automated pytest checks pin the dataset, the parquet
schemas, the model-wrapper outputs, and the statistical-appendix
artefacts at every published commit.

\subsection{Ablation~A --- Hijri-feature injection}
\label{sec:results:ablation_a}

\Cref{fig:regime_pair}(b) summarises the central finding of
Ablation~A: the \emph{same} Hijri features have opposite effects
on Ramadan MAE depending on the injection mechanism. Three
response classes emerge:

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
  \item \textbf{Helps when injected via feature
    engineering or residual head.} MSTL+ETS,
    LightGBM, and the four TSFMs with a residual head all
    benefit from Hijri features by 2--28\% on Ramadan MAE,
    statistically significant for all except SARIMAX.
  \item \textbf{No detectable effect.} SARIMAX (-0.2\%) and
    PatchTSMixer with the features added as cross-channels
    (+4.0\%, not significant) show no material change.
  \item \textbf{Hurts when injected via HuggingFace covariate
    API.} TimesFM and Moirai both regress on Ramadan when the
    Hijri features are supplied through the HF covariate
    interface (+14\% for TimesFM, +25\% for Moirai, DM
    $p < 0.001$).
\end{itemize}

The mechanism is interpretable: HuggingFace's covariate path
appends the feature channels to the model's attention context,
where the pretrained model has no prior over how to weight them
relative to the load history. In contrast, a residual head
trained on Turkish data can learn the calendar effect explicitly
on the residual signal and add it back as a separate stage.


\subsection{Ablation~B --- Compound regime}
\label{sec:results:ablation_b}

The Compound regime is structurally empty for the 2018--2025
window, as anticipated in \Cref{sec:data:regimes}. Concretely:

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
  \item Ramadan windows in 2018--2025 fall in March--June.
  \item Heat-wave events (southern temperature $\geq$
    \SI{35}{\celsius} for $\geq 3$ consecutive days) fall in
    June--August.
  \item Overlap windows (early June) never meet the three-day
    threshold.
\end{itemize}

Consequently, the two
interaction features \texttt{ramadan\_x\_heatwave} and
\texttt{ramadan\_x\_temp\_above\_35} are identically zero in
train, validation, and test. The
\texttt{hijri\_plusB} variant of SARIMAX produces predictions
statistically indistinguishable from the \texttt{hijri} variant
(maximum element-wise difference \num{0.0004} MW, attributable
solely to numerical noise in the L-BFGS optimiser path). The same
applies for the LightGBM and PatchTSMixer \texttt{hijri\_plusB}
variants. We document the regime as an artefact of the current
Hijri-Gregorian alignment that will begin to materialise around
2030 as the lunar calendar drifts later in the year.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Interpretation and deployment}

The composite-system progression from a single-model baseline at
MAE \num{968.9} (Chronos-Bolt-Base bare) to a meta-router at MAE
\num{838.8} is a $13.4\%$ headline reduction with three notable
properties: it is \emph{compounded} (about 60\% of the gain comes
from per-regime specialists and 40\% from ensembling them where
their CIs overlap), \emph{transparent} (every component is
independently auditable and replaceable, unlike an end-to-end
fine-tuned neural network), and \emph{deployable} (no GPU
fine-tuning, no new pretraining data, and the operational
day-ahead inference cost is dominated by a single Chronos forward
pass that can be pre-computed and cached).

Ablation~A is the most theoretically interesting result. Across
two distinct TSFM architectures (TimesFM, Moirai), injecting the
\emph{same} Hijri features through HuggingFace's in-band covariate
API \emph{hurts} Ramadan MAE by 14--25\%, while injecting them
through a post-hoc LightGBM residual head \emph{helps} the same
models by 9--22\%. The mechanism is interpretable: the covariate
API appends extra channels into a pretrained attention context
that has no prior over them, where they function as distractors;
the residual head bypasses that entirely by learning the calendar
effect on the residual signal and adding it back as a separate
correction stage. The implication for the foundation-model
deployment community is general: domain-specific covariates whose
distribution is unlike anything in the pretraining corpus should
be applied as \emph{external} corrections rather than
\emph{internal} model inputs, and we expect this asymmetry to
extend beyond the Hijri calendar to any structured covariate
that is non-stationary across pretraining and deployment domains.
The corollary explains why even the tuned tabular incumbent
LightGBM-hijri --- trained on essentially the v2 feature set the
residual head uses --- still gains $4\%$ from a residual head on
its own residuals: the regime-stratified routing converts an
all-regime model into a Normal+Ramadan specialist for the residual
stage, and the L1 residual target with different hyperparameters
captures nonlinear interactions the direct-prediction objective
failed to extract.

For operators, the headline deployment is the meta-router-v2 (MAE
\num{838.8}); a single-call alternative is the routed
best-per-regime composite (MAE \num{916.0}, one model per $\tau$);
the GPU-free path is LightGBM-hijri + residual head (MAE
\num{940.4}); and for short-horizon ($1$--$6\,$h) sub-day use,
Time-MoE-200M $L = 720$ should ship regardless of the headline
choice, as its $h = 1$ MAE of $262$ is roughly four times better
than the next-best model at that horizon.

\subsection{Limitations and threats to validity}

Four constraints temper the headline claims. (i)~\textbf{Distribution
drift:} the 2024--2025 test window is structurally newer than the
2018--2023 train+val window (the COVID-19 era sits inside training),
and several trained models exhibit a substantial val-to-test MAE
gap --- PatchTSMixer's val MAE of \num{545} vs.\ test MAE of
\num{1556} is the most dramatic. Zero-shot TSFMs are immune to
this; the trained models are not, and aggressive fine-tuning
inherits the risk. (ii)~\textbf{Single-seed PatchTSMixer:} resource
constraints limited evaluation to one random seed per variant at
$L = 168$ and an L-probe over $L \in \{96, 168\}$ only; a full
$5 \times 3 \times 4$ grid would provide variance estimates.
(iii)~\textbf{Compound-regime emptiness:} Ablation~B is
structurally untestable on the 2018--2025 window; the calendar
alignment relaxes around 2030. (iv)~\textbf{In-test residual
training:} residual heads are fit via 3-fold time-block CV
\emph{within} the test window rather than on a held-out 2018--2023
historical TSFM-error trajectory. This is a practical concession
(retrieving train+val TSFM predictions required additional GPU
inference time that was deferred); a production deployment would
train on the longer span, which we expect to widen the rescue
effect modestly, particularly for the strong TSFMs that currently
see only $\sim$\num{7300} hours per residual-training fold.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

This 31-system benchmark of day-ahead Turkish load forecasting
delivers two contributions. The \emph{headline} contribution is a
meta-router composite (MAE \num{838.8}, 95\% CI [\num{750.8},
\num{948.7}]) combining four residual-corrected models for Normal
hours, LightGBM-hijri for Ramadan, and a Heat-wave ensemble of
Chronos-Bolt and Time-MoE --- a $13.4\%$ improvement over the
strongest single bare model and $14.3\%$ over the tuned
LightGBM-hijri tabular incumbent. The \emph{methodological}
contribution is the systematic demonstration that post-hoc
LightGBM residual correction with regime-stratified routing
reliably improves every base model by a margin that scales
monotonically with the bare model's weakness ($2\%$ on Chronos,
$48\%$ on SARIMAX), together with the Ablation~A finding that
identical Hijri features \emph{hurt} TSFMs by 14--25\% through
HuggingFace's in-band covariate API but \emph{help} the same
models by 9--22\% through a post-hoc residual head --- clean
empirical support for post-hoc correction as a default mechanism
for injecting domain-specific calendar structure into zero-shot
foundation models. Three open directions remain:
fine-tuning Chronos-Bolt on Turkish data (script shipped at
\texttt{scripts/finetune\_chronos.py}; rationale for deferral at
\texttt{docs/chronos\_finetune.md}), retraining residual heads on
the full 2018--2023 historical-TSFM-error span instead of the
in-test 3-fold short-cut, and adding a dedicated holiday-aware
second-stage corrector for the five universal failure days that
dominate residual test error. The complete benchmark is
reproducible via the manifest at
\texttt{docs/reproducibility.md} ($\sim$8 h GPU + 5 h CPU on a
local RTX 4070 Laptop), pinned by 152 automated pytest checks.
